watchlab

Quality-adjusted price indices and supply-side metrics for wristwatch references.

What these numbers are. Everything here is derived from asking prices, not transactions. A listing disappearing is not a sale. The index controls for condition, papers, box, age, country and seller type so it is not fooled by a changing listing mix — but it still measures what sellers ask, not what buyers pay. Treat it as a screen that tells you where to look, never as a return forecast.

Constant-quality index

Hedonic index vs the naive median it corrects. Where the two diverge, the gap is composition drift — a changing mix of listings masquerading as price movement.

Transaction-based index (auctions)

Same hedonic-vs-naive comparison, but on realised auction prices instead of asks — the one number here that reflects what a buyer actually paid. Quarterly periods: auction volume for one reference is a fraction of listing volume, so monthly bins would be too thin to estimate reliably.

Genetic strategy screen (experimental)

Evolves a weight vector over the same supply-side features above (plus price momentum), walk-forward validated: fit on earlier checkpoints, scored unseen on later ones, always reported next to equal-weight buy-and-hold on the identical out-of-sample window. This is not a trading strategy — watches aren't day-tradeable, a single round trip already costs ~18–20% — it's a GA-tuned generalisation of the supply-side score above. Per this project's own design notes, buy-and-hold usually wins that comparison, and this panel will say so plainly when it does.

Glossary

Every abbreviation and metric used above, in one place. Hover a column header anywhere for a one-line reminder.

Supply-side screen

Score
Composite of the metrics below, scaled 0–1 where 1 reads “tight market”. A screen, not a return forecast.
Live
Number of listings currently for sale for this reference.
Median ask
Median asking price across live listings, converted to EUR. An ask, not a transaction.
Days on mkt / DOM
Median days a listing sat before it left the market. The data can't tell you whether it sold, was delisted, or expired — treat it as a liquidity proxy, never a sale record.
Cut rate
Share of listings that reduced their asking price at least once, measured against each listing's own peak ask.
Cut depth
Among listings that did cut their price, the median size of that reduction.
Dispersion
Inter-quartile range of asking prices divided by the median. Higher means sellers disagree more about what the watch is worth.
New/gone
Churn ratio: new listings divided by delistings over the window. Above 1 = supply growing; below 1 = supply draining.
Cost hurdle
Gross price rise needed over a year just to break even on a full round trip (buy + sell), given this project's ~18–20% friction assumption.

Index panels

Gross CAGR
Annualised growth rate of the quality-adjusted (hedonic) index, before costs.
Net of costs
The same CAGR after subtracting round-trip friction (fees, spread, shipping/insurance).
Papers premium
Estimated price premium for a listing sold with papers, relative to one without, from the index model.
Fit / R²
Share of price variation the model explains. Not purely a quality signal — a very homogeneous set of listings can also produce a high R².
n / n obs
Number of observations (fresh listings, or auction sales) the index was fit on.
Houses
Distinct auction houses contributing sales to the transaction-based index.

Genetic strategy screen

Top K held
How many references the evolved screen selects to (hypothetically) hold each period.
Checkpoints (train/test)
Points in time the screen is evaluated at. Weights are evolved against train checkpoints only; test checkpoints are held out and never seen during evolution — that split is what “walk-forward” means.
Fitness
The GA's internal score per candidate: mean net return on train checkpoints, minus a penalty for a large weight vector.
Weights
How much each feature counts toward a reference's score, and in which direction — the sign is learned, not assumed.
Momentum
Trailing change in the hedonic index over the previous checkpoint interval — the one weight that's price-based rather than supply-side.
Buy-and-hold
The baseline the GA is graded against: equal-weight across the whole universe, no selection at all. Per this project's own design notes, it usually wins.
